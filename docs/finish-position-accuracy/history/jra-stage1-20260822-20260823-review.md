## Direct expert-routing follow-up

The follow-up implemented the router as a counterfactual-utility gate rather
than an upset-probability gate. This follows adaptive mixtures of local experts
and learning-to-defer work: estimate the incremental utility of each expert,
then defer only when its predicted advantage clears a fixed per-race margin.
The target was the mean paired change in cumulative Top1, Top2, and Top3 hits.
The feature set contained 90 pre-race race/field/model-disagreement signals.

Strict forward-only champion and market-free Stage-1 scores were generated for
2018-2022, expanding router training from 3,459 races to about 20,700 races.
Candidate selection used expanding-window validation
2018-2020→2021 through 2018-2023→2024. A robust candidate had to keep aggregate
Top1-Top5 nonnegative, improve the overall/favorite-driven/upset Top1-Top3
objective, and have a positive objective in at least three of four validation
years for every segment.

The two-expert campaign (champion versus market-free Stage-1) produced no
robustly passing loop. A 2024-only selection would have chosen loop 46 at
+0.106pp overall, +0.057pp favorite-driven, and +0.210pp upset, but blind 2025
was -0.202pp overall and -0.294pp upset. Paired race-date cluster bootstrap
95% intervals were entirely below zero for overall and upset, demonstrating
why the earlier single-year rule was rejected. Under rolling selection, the
fallback loop 7 was positive in only one of four upset folds and was -0.010pp
overall on blind 2025.

A second 50-loop campaign added the final-market favorite as a third expert
and learned its champion-relative utility separately. All 50 loops failed the
rolling gate. The least-bad loop 25 was -0.039pp overall, -0.051pp
favorite-driven, and -0.014pp upset over 2021-2024; blind 2025 was -0.029pp
overall (paired cluster-bootstrap 95% interval -0.144pp to +0.067pp). The
favorite expert largely duplicated information already present in the
champion, so erroneous deferrals cost more than the rare gains.

The reproducible implementation and report are
`apps/pc-keiba-viewer/tmp/candidate-jra-expert-router-50-2026-08-24/utility_router_experiment.py`
and `utility_router_report.json`. The statistical design was informed by
Jacobs et al. (1991, adaptive mixtures of local experts), Mozannar and Sontag
(2020, consistent learning to defer), Verma et al. (2023, deferral to multiple
experts), Bergmeir et al. (2018, time-series cross-validation), and Hansen
(2005, data-snooping-aware model comparison).

**Decision:** neither direct router is production eligible. The failed
candidate remains an experiment only; aliases and serving behavior are
unchanged. Further routing work first needs genuinely diverse experts (for
example distance/surface/pace specialists) and sufficiently frequent
disagreement wins, then the same rolling and blind gates.

## Surface-specialist dynamic market blend

The next campaign created the missing expert diversity rather than continuing
to tune a gate over nearly identical models. For every 2018-2025 validation
year, turf, dirt, and obstacle CatBoost experts were trained only on earlier
races of the matching surface. Both the 250-feature market-aware feature set
and 235-feature market-free feature set were trained. The 2025 market-aware
surface expert improved the global champion at every reported cutoff before
routing: Top1 33.40% versus 33.00%, Top2 53.02% versus 52.79%, Top3 65.76%
versus 65.59%, Top4 74.24% versus 74.15%, and Top5 81.04% versus 80.96%.

Fifty continuous-routing hypotheses then varied the lower market-aware weight,
upset-probability activation threshold, and response curve. The fixed routing
family uses the surface experts in turf intermediate, dirt sprint, and
obstacle extended races, plus high-upset-probability turf mile/long races.
Within those contexts, market dependence changes continuously as

`w_market = 1 - (1 - w_min) * clip((p_upset - threshold) / (1 - threshold), 0, 1)^gamma`.

The selection rule first required 2024 Top1-Top5 nonregression and positive
Top1-Top3 mean changes overall and in both outcome segments. It then applied a
conservative shrinkage preference: choose the largest minimum market weight
before maximizing the weaker segment. This selected loop 43
(`w_min=0.95`, threshold `0.40`, gamma `0.5`), not the largest apparent 2024
gain. The classifier diagnostics were AUC 0.6582, Brier 0.2033, and 10-bin ECE
0.0175 in 2024; AUC 0.6612, Brier 0.2051, and ECE 0.0116 in 2025.

### Forward shadow implementation

The 2026 shadow artifact bundle was fixed after selection and trained only
through 2025. It contains six CatBoost rankers (turf/dirt/obstacle, each with
market-aware 250-feature and market-free 235-feature variants) plus one binary
CatBoost upset classifier. The classifier is trained on 27,639 forward OOF race
rows from 2018-2025 using the exact 14-feature order recorded in its metadata.
Every model and metadata sidecar is registered in
`production-artifacts.json`; the local selector closure reports `MATCH` for 40
selected files, including SHA-256 and byte-size validation for all 14 new files.
The Dockerfile's two-stage artifact path was also reproduced locally: the full
183-file source tree staged 36 reachable finish-position files (74 MiB), the
staged-only verifier returned `MATCH` with no findings, and all seven shadow
CatBoost artifacts loaded from that staged tree with the expected 235/250
feature contracts. A literal Docker image build was not possible in this
session because the local Docker daemon was not running; the source-to-staged
closure and runtime model-loading portions of that build were nevertheless
executed directly.

`predict_upcoming.score_dynamic_market_shadow` loads the fixed bundle,
recomputes the global Champion and market-free signals, selects the matching
surface experts, calculates the pre-race upset probability and loop-43 weight,
then reuses those model outputs to rank all 50 fixed routing hypotheses. The
classifier and four expert score arrays are inferred once per race; only the
lightweight blend parameters vary across loops 1-50. Its baseline is extracted from the exact
served UPSERT rows produced in the same in-memory scoring call, not from a
second Champion score. Each record also carries the served model version, a
SHA-256 of the ordered feature rows, and comparison contract
`served-same-snapshot/v1`. Legacy rows without that contract are excluded by
the MLflow query. Any shadow load, score, or database failure is caught
without changing or blocking the served rows. The separate
`finish_position_dynamic_market_shadow_predictions` table records:

- race, router/model versions, and the same-snapshot comparison contract;
- upset probability, market weight, selected context, and route reason;
- baseline Top1-Top5 and shadow Top1-Top5 horse identifiers.

The same module fixes the post-result metric contract through
`evaluate_shadow_outcomes`: cumulative Top1, Top2, Top3, Top4, and Top5 are
always returned for all races, favorite-driven races, and upset races. An
empty segment is reported as `None`, never as a fabricated zero.

`mlflow_tracking.dynamic_market_shadow_eval` performs the operational join
against finalized `jvd_se` rows and records those same cumulative Top1-Top5
metrics in the separate `finish-position/dynamic-market-shadow` experiment.
The final winner's `tansho_ninkijun` defines the outcome segment (rank 1-3 is
favorite-driven and rank 4+ is upset); tied winners use the minimum final
market rank, while `00`/missing popularity stays outside both segment
denominators rather than being guessed. The daily MLflow launchd job revisits
a trailing 90-day window and updates one idempotent run per date and immutable
router version, so late-arriving results are repaired without duplicate runs.
The daily job evaluates all 50 router versions and logs one cumulative run per
version for the window. Each run adds the paired
Top1-Top3 mean change, date-cluster bootstrap 95% intervals for every Top1-Top5
delta, and an explicit promotion gate. Promotion requires overall Top1-Top5
nonregression, positive overall/favorite-driven/upset Top1-Top3 means, at least
31 independent race dates, and a strictly positive family-wise 95% lower bound
for all three Top1-Top3 means. The promotion lower bound uses a one-sided
Bonferroni threshold of 0.05/50 = 0.001 across the 50 hypotheses; ordinary 95%
intervals remain available for description but cannot promote a model.
Resamples in which a segment is absent do not produce a misleading interval.
No 2026-08-24 finalized JRA rows currently exist for an actual forward score.

### Mathematical and paper-informed review

Dynamic race routing is a mixture-of-experts problem: the upset classifier is
the gate and the market-aware / market-free rankers are experts. This matches
the original formulation in which a gate assigns cases to specialized experts
rather than assuming one expert is uniformly best
([Jacobs et al., 1991](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~hinton/absps/jjnh91.pdf)).
That paper supports the architecture, but it is not accuracy evidence for this
dataset; only the paired production comparison provides that evidence.

The evaluation is paired by race, while uncertainty is resampled by race date
so all same-day races move together. This preserves possible within-day
dependence, consistent with the cluster-bootstrap analysis of Field and Welsh
([2007](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00593.x)).
The earlier eight-date minimum was too weak: Cameron, Gelbach, and Miller show
that ordinary clustered asymptotics can over-reject with 5–30 clusters
([2008](https://www.nber.org/papers/t0344)). The promotion gate therefore
requires 31 dates as well as positive percentile-bootstrap lower bounds. The
date-ordered forward design also avoids treating temporally dependent rows as
ordinary exchangeable folds; Bergmeir, Hyndman, and Koo explicitly note that
time-series validation requires assumptions about serial correlation
([2018](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/cv-wp.pdf)). No repeated-split paired
t-test is used: Dietterich documented inflated type-I error for common
classifier-comparison tests based on repeated random splits
([1998](https://doi.org/10.1162/089976698300017197)).

The promotion hypothesis is an intersection: all Top1–Top5 point deltas must
be nonnegative, and the Top1–Top3 mean must have a strictly positive
Bonferroni-adjusted family-wise 95% lower bound in all, favorite-driven, and
upset segments. Failure of any component rejects deployment. With only the
2026-08-23/24 production facts, this gate cannot pass and no accuracy
improvement may be claimed.

A native runtime smoke on 2025 race `jra:2025:0106:06:07` loaded all seven
artifacts, reproduced a probability of 0.171647 and market weight 1.0, and
generated identical baseline/shadow Top5 for that low-upset turf-intermediate
case. This proves artifact loading, feature projection, classifier inference,
routing, and ranking execute together; it is an implementation smoke, not new
accuracy evidence.

**Decision:** retain loop 43 as the forward shadow candidate. The code and
artifact closure are ready for the next container deployment, while production
serving ranks remain unchanged. Promotion from shadow requires a genuinely
future sample with positive Top1-Top5 behavior and a positive upset-segment
Top1-Top3 effect. The missing finalized 2026-08-24 JRA rows prevent that
confirmation from being manufactured from the requested date.

## Result and next improvement direction

The Stage-1 Top1 swap remains a statistically positive walk-forward research
result, but is not a demonstrated production improvement and is not live. The
new loop-43 shadow candidate also improves every
Top1-Top5 point estimate in retrospective 2025 while dynamically reducing
market dependence, but it is not deployed because its upset interval includes
zero and future 2026 confirmation data is not yet available. Neither change
repairs the more valuable Stage-2 serving-input gap.
The healthy-market blind benchmark remains approximately 33.63% Top1 for the
250-feature champion versus 28.89% for market-free Stage-1, but the champion
collapses when its market inputs are absent. The next improvement should be an
immutable, race-timestamped odds/feature snapshot plus timely rescore that
recomputes all champion market features. It must shadow-log board completeness,
gate reason, model choice, and feature-cache identity before changing selection.

Any candidate must first reproduce the MLflow production baseline on the same
race rows and feature snapshots, then beat it before deployment. The two-day R2
cache result must remain a monitoring caveat, not be retrofitted into a post-hoc
routing rule.

## Literal August 23/24 two-day OOF check

The available fold-safe dataset containing both requested calendar dates is 2025. It has exactly 36 races on 2025-08-23 and 36 races on 2025-08-24. This is
a research OOF comparison and is not represented as the MLflow production

## Cloudflare weather synchronization and routing check

The production weather Worker was deployed as version
`9d6ebac2-731b-4bd5-9410-a8187a9b6085`. Its R2 read path now selects the
`actual` object per venue when both forecast and actual objects exist, while
retaining forecast only for venues whose actual object has not arrived. The
queue consumer rejects incomplete 24-hour payloads, retries five times at
60-second intervals, and sends exhausted jobs to
`venue-weather-dead-letter`. Post-deployment public API checks returned exactly
600 rows, 25 venues, and 600 unique venue-hour keys for both 2026-08-23 and
2026-08-24. The queue API independently reported `max_retries=5`,
`retry_delay=60`, and the configured dead-letter queue.

The local differential sync queried Cloudflare R2 SQL by date and preferred
actual/newest rows for each venue-hour key. A 2025-08-23 through 2025-08-24
control sync read 1,200 rows and found all 1,200 unchanged. The missing local
2026-07-12 through 2026-08-24 interval then read 26,400 Cloudflare rows and
inserted all 26,400, with zero updates and zero unchanged rows. The local 2026
DuckDB therefore contains the Cloudflare differential rather than a separately
generated weather source.

The prediction-container client change adds three bounded fetch attempts,
exponential delay, a complete 25-venue × 24-hour response check, and a primary
key upsert. The full package suite passed 1,990 tests with one skip and 98.06%
coverage. A Colima image build passed the 38-artifact integrity contract, and
an in-container fetch stored 600 Cloudflare rows as 600 unique keys across 25
venues. This container image was not deployed because the shared Worker deploy
would also include unrelated dirty-tree changes; the weather Worker deployment
above is the only production mutation in this check.

For the requested accuracy evaluation, daily weather was aggregated over
09:00–17:00, matching the production feature-builder interval. The upset
classifier added mean/max temperature, precipitation, wind speed and gusts,
plus hours at or above 28°C and hours with precipitation. The heat threshold is
paper-informed feature engineering only: the reviewed equine heat-illness paper
supports physiological relevance, not a finishing-position accuracy claim.
Training used 24,184 races from 2018–2024; 2025 remained blind for classifier
fitting. Weather reduced 2025 upset-classifier AUC from 0.661810 to 0.658733
(`-0.003077`).

On 2025-08-23, 12 of the same 50 probability-gated hypotheses passed the point
gate and loop 9 was selected. On the one-shot 2025-08-24 confirmation, its
Top1–Top5 deltas were again -5.556, -5.556, -2.778, 0.000, and -2.778 points;
Top1–Top3 mean deltas were -4.630 overall, -6.349 for favorite-driven races,
and -2.222 for upset races. The weather-aware router is therefore rejected and
was not deployed.

Two follow-up combinations tested whether the daily aggregation was hiding the
signal. First, each race was joined to the Cloudflare observation at its exact
JVD `hasso_jikoku` hour, then weather was interacted with turf/dirt surface,
distance, and track condition. Its 2025 upset AUC was 0.658850, still below the
weather-free 0.661810. No 2025-08-23 hypothesis passed; the diagnostic fallback
lost -8.333, -11.111, -8.333, -2.778, and -5.556 points at Top1–Top5 on
2025-08-24.

Second, the same exact-hour interactions were added to the counterfactual
utility router, where the target is the relative Top1–Top3 gain from selecting
Stage-1 or the market favorite instead of the champion. All 50 rolling
hypotheses failed the robust gate. Its selected loop 15 changed 0.571% of
rolling races. On blind 2025 its Top1–Top3 mean estimates were +0.0193 points
overall, +0.0287 points favorite-driven, and 0.0000 points upset; the respective
ordinary 95% intervals were [-0.0765, +0.1251], [-0.1145, +0.1840], and
[0.0000, 0.0000] points. It switched no race on either 2025-08-23 or
2025-08-24, so all literal two-day deltas were zero. These results reject both
the alignment/interactions explanation and the alternative utility target for
the tested feature family; they do not establish that every possible weather
representation is useless.

### Strictly pre-race weather-window follow-up

The next test excluded the post-time hour and all later observations. Each
race required six complete venue-hour observations immediately before its JVD
post hour. The 50 fixed hypotheses combined ten pre-race regimes based on the
prior-three/prior-six-hour temperature level and trend, cumulative
precipitation, and maximum gust with five candidate-versus-baseline track-going
aptitude thresholds. Selection used 2023–2024; 2025 was the confirmation year.

Thirty hypotheses passed the 2023–2024 point gate. The selected loop 6 switched
only when the mean temperature over the three strictly preceding hourly
observations was below 28°C; it imposed no additional aptitude restriction. On
2025's 3,455 races it made 189 switches. Its cumulative Top1–Top5 changes were
+0.550, +0.868, +0.666, +0.521, and +0.579 percentage points. Top1–Top3 mean
changes were +0.695 points overall, +0.689 in favorite-driven races, and
+0.705 in upset races. The paired candidate-only versus baseline-only hit
counts were 47–28, 69–39, 64–41, 59–41, and 51–31 for Top1 through Top5.

A 20,000-repeat race-date cluster bootstrap over 109 dates gave ordinary 95%
intervals of [+0.217, +1.188] points overall, [+0.070, +1.328] in
favorite-driven races, and [+0.057, +1.396] in upset races for the Top1–Top3
mean. However, the one-sided 0.001 lower bounds required by the 50-hypothesis
family-wise promotion gate were -0.029, -0.288, and -0.298 points. The ordinary
gate therefore passed but the family-wise gate failed.

The literal 2025-08-23 selection chose the prior-three-hour wet regime. On the
untouched 2025-08-24 date it switched one race: Top4 improved by +2.778 points
while Top1, Top2, Top3, and Top5 were unchanged. This one-date confirmation did
not pass the strict point gate and cannot provide cluster-level inference.

The next NAR evaluation removed the archived-model approximation. SHA-256

0.00, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, and 1.00. Its 18 inference-time cell types covered
venue, distance, surface, going, season, field size, market concentration,
model disagreement, rank disagreement, Top-3 overlap, current-model margin,

wind, and gust regimes. Single cells and two-cell intersections used a
conservative lower-gain rule with the current 0.50 blend as hierarchical
fallback. Fifty-four minimum-size, lower-bound, and hierarchy-depth
configurations were evaluated by rolling time: 2023 selected rules for 2024,
then 2023–2024 selected rules for 2025. This separation is out-of-time for the
router decisions, not for the two underlying production artifacts: those
artifacts were fit through 2025. Consequently the 2024–2025 absolute accuracy

### NAR composite-cell per-cell weight optimization

The subsequent router prohibited a single NAR-wide fusion weight. Every race
was assigned at minimum to a venue cell, and deeper composite cells managed
their component conditions, parent cell, learning-year range, training-race
count, and selected weight in one rule record. The weight grid contained 21
XGB/Transformer score-z weights from 0.00 through 1.00 in 0.05 increments.
Six predeclared hierarchies combined venue with season, strictly pre-race
temperature and rain, model disagreement, market concentration, rank
disagreement, distance, surface, going, field size, wind, and gust cells.

The first 216-configuration pass optimized overall Top1–Top3 utility and was
rejected because it improved upset races while consistently reducing
favorite-driven accuracy. The objective was then corrected to maximize the
minimum conservative gain across overall, favorite-driven, and upset segments
inside each composite cell. Rolling selection again used 2023 for 2024 and
2023–2024 for 2025. Two configurations passed the pooled development gate
with the established -0.05 point per-year non-regression floor.

The selected hierarchy was venue → season → prior-three-hour temperature,
with at least 50 training races per deeper cell and a 1.28 standard-error
lower bound. On rolling 2024–2025, Top1–Top5 deltas were +0.0553, +0.0701,
+0.0738, +0.0590, and +0.0480 points. Top1–Top3 means were +0.0664 overall,
+0.0127 favorite-driven, and +0.2520 upset. The final fit contained 59
composite rules; the 2026 card used 13 distinct weights, so the global-weight
prohibition was satisfied in execution rather than only in configuration.

On the one-shot 2026 final set of 5,409 races, Top1–Top3 means remained
positive: +0.1233 points overall, +0.0866 favorite-driven, and +0.2553 upset.
The date-cluster 95% intervals were [+0.0372, +0.2132], [-0.0079, +0.1822],
and [+0.0816, +0.4606], respectively. However, cumulative Top4 and Top5
regressed by -0.0924 and -0.0555 points. The favorite-driven interval also
included zero. The strict promotion gate therefore failed; the composite-cell
router remains a research artifact and no production deployment was made.
