## Fifty-loop dynamic market-dependence campaign

A separate campaign tested whether healthy-board JRA races should use a
race-specific blend of the 250-feature champion and the 235-feature
market-free Stage-1 model. “Upset” was fixed before selection as an actual
winner with final market rank 4 or worse. The race classifier used only
pre-race market shape and model-disagreement/confidence inputs; its training
and evaluation were forward-only:

- 2023 trained the classifier used on the 2024 selection fold;
- 2023-2024 trained the classifier used on the untouched 2025 confirmation
  fold;
- no August 2026 outcome was used to train or select a candidate.

The classifier had real but insufficient discrimination: AUC 0.6492 on 2024
and 0.6558 on 2025, against actual upset rates 32.23% and 33.00%. Fifty unique
hypotheses crossed five low champion weights, five high champion weights, and
two response curves. Every loop reported cumulative Top1 through Top5 for the
whole fold and separately for favorite-driven and upset outcomes.

| 2024 best dynamic loop 39 | 3,454 | 34.366% | 54.343% | 66.532% | 75.101% | 81.413% |

accuracy improvement remains the market-free Stage-1 Top1 swap described
above. A future dynamic router must predict _which expert selects the actual
winner_, not merely whether the race is likely to be an upset, and must clear
the same Top1-Top5 plus both-outcome-segment gate before alias or routing
changes.

## Direct expert-routing follow-up

The follow-up implemented the router as a counterfactual-utility gate rather
than an upset-probability gate. This follows adaptive mixtures of local experts
and learning-to-defer work: estimate the incremental utility of each expert,
then defer only when its predicted advantage clears a fixed per-race margin.
The target was the mean paired change in cumulative Top1, Top2, and Top3 hits.
The feature set contained 90 pre-race race/field/model-disagreement signals.

Strict forward-only champion and market-free Stage-1 scores were generated for
2018-2022, expanding router training from 3,459 races to about 20,700 races.
Candidate selection used expanding-window validation
2018-2020→2021 through 2018-2023→2024. A robust candidate had to keep aggregate
Top1-Top5 nonnegative, improve the overall/favorite-driven/upset Top1-Top3
objective, and have a positive objective in at least three of four validation
years for every segment.

The two-expert campaign (champion versus market-free Stage-1) produced no
robustly passing loop. A 2024-only selection would have chosen loop 46 at
+0.106pp overall, +0.057pp favorite-driven, and +0.210pp upset, but blind 2025
was -0.202pp overall and -0.294pp upset. Paired race-date cluster bootstrap
95% intervals were entirely below zero for overall and upset, demonstrating
why the earlier single-year rule was rejected. Under rolling selection, the
fallback loop 7 was positive in only one of four upset folds and was -0.010pp
overall on blind 2025.

A second 50-loop campaign added the final-market favorite as a third expert
and learned its champion-relative utility separately. All 50 loops failed the
rolling gate. The least-bad loop 25 was -0.039pp overall, -0.051pp
favorite-driven, and -0.014pp upset over 2021-2024; blind 2025 was -0.029pp
overall (paired cluster-bootstrap 95% interval -0.144pp to +0.067pp). The
favorite expert largely duplicated information already present in the
champion, so erroneous deferrals cost more than the rare gains.

The reproducible implementation and report are
`apps/pc-keiba-viewer/tmp/candidate-jra-expert-router-50-2026-08-24/utility_router_experiment.py`
and `utility_router_report.json`. The statistical design was informed by
Jacobs et al. (1991, adaptive mixtures of local experts), Mozannar and Sontag
(2020, consistent learning to defer), Verma et al. (2023, deferral to multiple
experts), Bergmeir et al. (2018, time-series cross-validation), and Hansen
(2005, data-snooping-aware model comparison).

**Decision:** neither direct router is production eligible. The failed
candidate remains an experiment only; aliases and serving behavior are
unchanged. Further routing work first needs genuinely diverse experts (for
example distance/surface/pace specialists) and sufficiently frequent
disagreement wins, then the same rolling and blind gates.

## Surface-specialist dynamic market blend

The next campaign created the missing expert diversity rather than continuing
to tune a gate over nearly identical models. For every 2018-2025 validation
year, turf, dirt, and obstacle CatBoost experts were trained only on earlier
races of the matching surface. Both the 250-feature market-aware feature set
and 235-feature market-free feature set were trained. The 2025 market-aware
surface expert improved the global champion at every reported cutoff before
routing: Top1 33.40% versus 33.00%, Top2 53.02% versus 52.79%, Top3 65.76%
versus 65.59%, Top4 74.24% versus 74.15%, and Top5 81.04% versus 80.96%.

Fifty continuous-routing hypotheses then varied the lower market-aware weight,
upset-probability activation threshold, and response curve. The fixed routing
family uses the surface experts in turf intermediate, dirt sprint, and
obstacle extended races, plus high-upset-probability turf mile/long races.
Within those contexts, market dependence changes continuously as

`w_market = 1 - (1 - w_min) * clip((p_upset - threshold) / (1 - threshold), 0, 1)^gamma`.

The selection rule first required 2024 Top1-Top5 nonregression and positive
Top1-Top3 mean changes overall and in both outcome segments. It then applied a
conservative shrinkage preference: choose the largest minimum market weight
before maximizing the weaker segment. This selected loop 43
(`w_min=0.95`, threshold `0.40`, gamma `0.5`), not the largest apparent 2024
gain. The classifier diagnostics were AUC 0.6582, Brier 0.2033, and 10-bin ECE
0.0175 in 2024; AUC 0.6612, Brier 0.2051, and ECE 0.0116 in 2025.

| Confirmation delta vs global champion |     Top1 |     Top2 |     Top3 |     Top4 |     Top5 |
| ------------------------------------- | -------: | -------: | -------: | -------: | -------: |
| 2024 selection                        | +0.232pp | +0.318pp | +0.261pp | +0.376pp | +0.405pp |
| 2025 confirmation                     | +0.550pp | +0.318pp | +0.376pp | +0.058pp | +0.203pp |

The 2025 Top1-Top3 mean delta was +0.415pp overall, +0.546pp in
favorite-driven outcomes, and +0.147pp in upset outcomes. Paired race-date
cluster-bootstrap 95% intervals were +0.048pp to +0.788pp overall, +0.056pp
to +1.056pp favorite-driven, and -0.279pp to +0.578pp upset. Thus all required
point estimates improve, and the overall/favorite-driven changes exclude zero,
but the smaller upset improvement remains statistically uncertain.

The confirmation must be described as retrospective rather than genuinely
blind because 2025 had already been inspected during preceding router
development. No alias or served prediction was changed. The pure routing rule
is implemented in
`apps/finish-position-predict-container/src/predict_lib/dynamic_market_router.py`;
its fixed parameters, context routing, continuous market weight, validation,
and score blending have full branch coverage. The reproducible experiment is

### Forward shadow implementation

The 2026 shadow artifact bundle was fixed after selection and trained only
through 2025. It contains six CatBoost rankers (turf/dirt/obstacle, each with
market-aware 250-feature and market-free 235-feature variants) plus one binary
CatBoost upset classifier. The classifier is trained on 27,639 forward OOF race
rows from 2018-2025 using the exact 14-feature order recorded in its metadata.
Every model and metadata sidecar is registered in
`production-artifacts.json`; the local selector closure reports `MATCH` for 40
selected files, including SHA-256 and byte-size validation for all 14 new files.
The Dockerfile's two-stage artifact path was also reproduced locally: the full
183-file source tree staged 36 reachable finish-position files (74 MiB), the
staged-only verifier returned `MATCH` with no findings, and all seven shadow
CatBoost artifacts loaded from that staged tree with the expected 235/250
feature contracts. A literal Docker image build was not possible in this
session because the local Docker daemon was not running; the source-to-staged
closure and runtime model-loading portions of that build were nevertheless
executed directly.

`predict_upcoming.score_dynamic_market_shadow` now loads the fixed bundle,
recomputes the global Champion and market-free signals, selects the matching
surface experts, calculates the pre-race upset probability and loop-43 weight,
and ranks the counterfactual Top1-Top5. It runs only after served predictions
have been persisted, and any shadow load, score, or database failure is caught
without changing or blocking the served rows. The separate
`finish_position_dynamic_market_shadow_predictions` table records:

- race and immutable router/model versions;
- upset probability, market weight, selected context, and route reason;
- baseline Top1-Top5 and shadow Top1-Top5 horse identifiers.

The same module fixes the post-result metric contract through
`evaluate_shadow_outcomes`: cumulative Top1, Top2, Top3, Top4, and Top5 are
always returned for all races, favorite-driven races, and upset races. An
empty segment is reported as `None`, never as a fabricated zero.

`mlflow_tracking.dynamic_market_shadow_eval` now performs the operational join
against finalized `jvd_se` rows and records those same cumulative Top1-Top5
metrics in the separate `finish-position/dynamic-market-shadow` experiment.
The final winner's `tansho_ninkijun` defines the outcome segment (rank 1-3 is
favorite-driven and rank 4+ is upset); tied winners use the minimum final
market rank, while `00`/missing popularity stays outside both segment
denominators rather than being guessed. The daily MLflow launchd job revisits
a trailing 90-day window and updates one idempotent run per date and immutable
router version, so late-arriving results are repaired without duplicate runs.
It also logs one cumulative run for the window. That run adds the paired
Top1-Top3 mean change, date-cluster bootstrap 95% intervals for every Top1-Top5
delta, and an explicit promotion gate. Promotion requires overall Top1-Top5
nonregression, positive overall/favorite-driven/upset Top1-Top3 means, at least
eight independent race dates, and a strictly positive 95% lower bound for all
three Top1-Top3 means. Resamples in which a segment is absent do not produce a
misleading interval for that segment.
No 2026-08-24 finalized JRA rows currently exist for an actual forward score.

A native runtime smoke on 2025 race `jra:2025:0106:06:07` loaded all seven
artifacts, reproduced a probability of 0.171647 and market weight 1.0, and
generated identical baseline/shadow Top5 for that low-upset turf-intermediate
case. This proves artifact loading, feature projection, classifier inference,
routing, and ranking execute together; it is an implementation smoke, not new
accuracy evidence.

**Decision:** retain loop 43 as the forward shadow candidate. The code and
artifact closure are ready for the next container deployment, while production
serving ranks remain unchanged. Promotion from shadow requires a genuinely
future sample with positive Top1-Top5 behavior and a positive upset-segment
Top1-Top3 effect. The missing finalized 2026-08-24 JRA rows prevent that
confirmation from being manufactured from the requested date.

## Result and next improvement direction

The Stage-1 Top1 swap remains a statistically supported, deliberately narrow
model improvement. The new loop-43 shadow candidate also improves every
Top1-Top5 point estimate in retrospective 2025 while dynamically reducing
market dependence, but it is not deployed because its upset interval includes
zero and future 2026 confirmation data is not yet available. Neither change
repairs the more valuable Stage-2 serving-input gap.
The healthy-market blind benchmark remains approximately 33.63% Top1 for the
250-feature champion versus 28.89% for market-free Stage-1, but the champion
collapses when its market inputs are absent. The next improvement should be an
immutable, race-timestamped odds/feature snapshot plus timely rescore that
recomputes all champion market features. It must shadow-log board completeness,
gate reason, model choice, and feature-cache identity before changing selection.

The candidate must be deployed and then measured on genuinely served future
races before its live effect is called confirmed. The two-day cache result must
remain a monitoring caveat, not be retrofitted into a post-hoc routing rule.
